Before we begin

You don't have to explain everything at once.

Sahara is a quiet way to stay in touch with the person supporting you. Check in when you can, in your own words, in your own language.

What this is

  • A way to tell your support worker how you are, without repeating your whole story.
  • A system that notices when things seem to be getting harder, and asks a person to look.
  • Yours — you choose what is shared, and you can change it any time.

What this is not

  • Not a therapist, and not a diagnosis. It never decides anything about your case.
  • Not an emergency service. In immediate danger, call 112.

Setting up takes about two minutes. Every question can be skipped.

Question 1 of 7

What should we call you?

This is only what appears on your screen and to your support worker.

It does not have to be your real name. Many people choose something only they would recognise.
Question 2 of 7

How old are you?

This decides which team supports you and what protections apply to your case.

Because you are under 18, your case is handled by the child protection team, an appropriate adult is involved, and some things must be reported by law. Your worker will explain exactly what that means before anything is shared.
Question 3 of 7

Who are you here as?

Witnesses and family members get different support from survivors, so it helps to know.

Question 4 of 7 · optional

What brings you here?

Only so the right support is offered. Choose as many as fit, or skip this entirely.

Voice check-ins are transcribed in this language. You can switch any time.
Question 5 of 7

When is it safe to reach you?

A call at the wrong moment can put you at risk. Your worker will only contact you inside this window.

Question 6 of 7 · optional

Set a safe word.

If you are ever being watched or forced, write your safe word in any check-in. The screen will look completely normal — but your worker is told immediately that you are not safe.

Pick an ordinary word you could type in a sentence without anyone noticing. Avoid anything obviously about danger.
What happens when you use it: nothing changes on your screen — you see the same ordinary "thank you" message. Behind it, the case is marked duress, your worker is alerted, and no notification is sent to your phone.
Question 7 of 7

What are you willing to share?

Nothing here is permanent. Change any of it later under Privacy.

Every time somebody opens your case it is written to a log you can read. Sahara never shares anything with police, courts or family without you turning it on here first.

            The pitch

            Detect → Explain → Connect → Intervene → Follow up.

            Not an AI therapist and not an automated decision-maker. A digital safety net that notices when a victim or witness may need help, explains why, hands the case to an authorised human, and verifies that the follow-up actually happened.

            Boundaries we hold

            • No diagnosis of PTSD, depression or suicidality from a message or a recording.
            • The AI never decides relocation, police involvement or legal outcomes.
            • The wording is always potential distress indicators, well-being trend, human review recommended.
            • Every case, name and quotation in this prototype is fictional.
