How it works

Four steps, and a person at the end of every one.

01

Check-in

A few short questions about different parts of life — sleep, safety, money, who they have spoken to. Some are spoken, some written. Skipping is always allowed.

02

Signal

Answers are read for potential distress indicators and compared with the weeks before. It is a direction of travel, never a diagnosis.

03

Human review

When signals accumulate, the case surfaces to its named caseworker with every reason listed and quoted. Nothing is decided automatically.

04

Support

The worker acts, records what was actually done, and later check-ins show whether it helped. The loop closes, or it stays open on the board.

Who it helps

Three people, one thread between them.

Survivors

Say how you are without retelling the whole story to a stranger. Choose when it is safe to be contacted. Hide the screen in one press if someone walks in.

Witnesses

People giving evidence are often forgotten between hearings. Prahari keeps track of the last time anyone actually spoke to them, and says so out loud.

Caseworkers

A caseload ordered by who needs attention today, with the reasoning shown rather than hidden — so the judgment stays yours and the paperwork writes itself.

Why it is different

Built around the ways people get missed.

Most systems wait for one dramatic signal. In practice people fall through the cracks quietly — a request nobody answered, a check-in nobody followed up, a call that was promised and never came.

Falling through the cracks

Tracks needs the survivor raised that nobody closed out — not just moments of crisis.

Nobody forgotten

Counts the days since a real human last made contact, and surfaces whoever has waited longest.

Barrier, not non-compliance

A missed check-in is read as a possible unmet need — no phone credit, no privacy at home — never as someone being difficult.

Explainable by design

Every point of priority traces back to a sentence the survivor actually wrote or said. No score appears without its reason.

Accountability both ways

The system records whether the promised support actually happened, and whether the person felt better afterwards.

Boundaries

What Prahari does, and what it will not do.

Judges and survivors deserve the same answer to this question, so it is written on the front page rather than buried in a policy.

Prahari does

  • Tracks longitudinal change against a survivor's own earlier check-ins
  • Detects explainable indicators, each traceable to a sentence she wrote or said
  • Identifies possible safety or distress escalation
  • Tracks unresolved needs nobody has closed out
  • Helps caseworkers prioritise follow-up across a caseload
  • Provides the evidence for why a case was flagged
  • Supports human intervention, and records whether it happened

Prahari does not

  • Diagnose mental illness
  • Replace psychologists or counsellors
  • Automatically dispatch police
  • Continuously monitor the survivor
  • Judge emotions from a camera or face
  • Make autonomous high-stakes decisions

AI assists. Humans decide.

What Prahari is not

Not a therapist. Not a decision-maker. A way of noticing, so that a person can arrive sooner.

Prahari never diagnoses a condition, and it never decides on relocation, police involvement or legal outcomes. It produces potential distress indicators, a well-being trend, and a recommendation that a human being takes a look.

Before we begin

You don't have to explain everything at once.

Prahari is a quiet way to stay in touch with the person supporting you. Check in when you can, in your own words, in your own language.

What this is

  • A way to tell your support worker how you are, without repeating your whole story.
  • A system that notices when things seem to be getting harder, and asks a person to look.
  • Yours — you choose what is shared, and you can change it any time.

What this is not

  • Not a therapist, and not a diagnosis. It never decides anything about your case.
  • Not an emergency service. In immediate danger, call 112.

Setting up takes about two minutes. Every question can be skipped.

Question 1 of 8

What should we call you?

This is only what appears on your screen and to your support worker.

It does not have to be your real name. Many people choose something only they would recognise.
Question 2 of 8

How old are you?

This decides which team supports you and what protections apply to your case.

Because you are under 18, your case is handled by the child protection team, an appropriate adult is involved, and some things must be reported by law. Your worker will explain exactly what that means before anything is shared.
Question 3 of 8

Who are you here as?

Witnesses and family members get different support from survivors, so it helps to know.

Question 4 of 8 · optional

What brings you here?

Only so the right support is offered. Choose as many as fit, or skip this entirely.

Voice answers are transcribed in this language. You can switch any time.
Question 5 of 8

When is it safe to reach you?

A call at the wrong moment can put you at risk. Your worker will only contact you inside this window.

Question 6 of 8 · optional

Set a safe word.

If you are ever being watched or forced, write your safe word in any answer. The screen will look completely normal — but your worker is told immediately that you are not safe.

Pick an ordinary word you could type in a sentence without anyone noticing. Avoid anything obviously about danger.
What happens when you use it: nothing changes on your screen — you see the same ordinary thank-you message. Behind it, the case is marked duress, your worker is alerted, and no notification is sent to your phone.
Question 7 of 8

What are you willing to share?

Nothing here is permanent. Change any of it later under Privacy.

Every time somebody opens your case it is written to a log you can read. Prahari never shares anything with police, courts or family without you turning it on here first.
Question 8 of 8

Choose how you sign in.

So your answers are here waiting for you next time, on any device.

Not your email and not your phone number — pick something that does not identify you.
Your data is safe with us. Your password is never stored — it is salted and stretched with PBKDF2-SHA512 over 120,000 rounds, and only that result is saved, so nobody can read it back, including us. Your answers stay on this server alone. There is no advertising code and no analytics code anywhere in Prahari, and nothing is ever passed to a third party.

            The pitch

            Detect → Explain → Connect → Intervene → Follow up.

            Not an AI therapist and not an automated decision-maker. A digital safety net that notices when a victim or witness may need help, explains why, hands the case to an authorised human, and verifies that the follow-up actually happened.

            Boundaries we hold

            • No diagnosis of PTSD, depression or suicidality from a message or a recording.
            • The AI never decides relocation, police involvement or legal outcomes.
            • The wording is always potential distress indicators, well-being trend, human review recommended.
            • Every case, name and quotation in this prototype is fictional.
