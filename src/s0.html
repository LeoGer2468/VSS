How it works

Four steps, and a person at the end of every one.

01

Check-in

A few short questions about different parts of life — sleep, safety, money, who they have spoken to. Some are spoken, some written. Skipping is always allowed.

02

Signal

Answers are read for potential distress indicators and compared with the weeks before. It is a direction of travel, never a diagnosis.

03

Human review

When signals accumulate, the case surfaces to its named caseworker with every reason listed and quoted. Nothing is decided automatically.

04

Support

The worker acts, records what was actually done, and later check-ins show whether it helped. The loop closes, or it stays open on the board.

Who it helps

Three people, one thread between them.

Survivors

Say how you are without retelling the whole story to a stranger. Choose when it is safe to be contacted. Hide the screen in one press if someone walks in.

Witnesses

People giving evidence are often forgotten between hearings. Prahari keeps track of the last time anyone actually spoke to them, and says so out loud.

Caseworkers

A caseload ordered by who needs attention today, with the reasoning shown rather than hidden — so the judgment stays yours and the paperwork writes itself.

Why it is different

Built around the ways people get missed.

Most systems wait for one dramatic signal. In practice people fall through the cracks quietly — a request nobody answered, a check-in nobody followed up, a call that was promised and never came.

Falling through the cracks

Tracks needs the survivor raised that nobody closed out — not just moments of crisis.

Nobody forgotten

Counts the days since a real human last made contact, and surfaces whoever has waited longest.

Barrier, not non-compliance

A missed check-in is read as a possible unmet need — no phone credit, no privacy at home — never as someone being difficult.

Explainable by design

Every point of priority traces back to a sentence the survivor actually wrote or said. No score appears without its reason.

Accountability both ways

The system records whether the promised support actually happened, and whether the person felt better afterwards.

What Prahari is not

Not a therapist. Not a decision-maker. A way of noticing, so that a person can arrive sooner.

Prahari never diagnoses a condition, and it never decides on relocation, police involvement or legal outcomes. It produces potential distress indicators, a well-being trend, and a recommendation that a human being takes a look.
